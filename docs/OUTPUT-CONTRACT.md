# Output contract

For two sufficiently readable required artifacts, `resume-opportunity-match` uses these headings in this stable order. A percentage score is not a substitute for evidence analysis.

## 1. Input quality and reading limitations

Inventory both artifacts and concrete limits that affect analysis. If either required artifact is absent or materially unreadable, this is the only section: name the gap, request a readable copy, and stop.

## 2. Role and sector identified

Identify the target role and sector from the opportunity. Ask only when the opportunity remains ambiguous.

## 3. Resume-to-opportunity relevance map

Map each material experience as relevant, partially relevant, or not relevant, with an evidence-based explanation.

## 4. Ten priority words or concepts

Extract the ten most material concepts. If the opportunity cannot support ten meaningful concepts, state the limit rather than pad the list.

## 5. Explicitly requested skills and inferred skills

Separate stated requirements from labeled recruiter inferences, citing opportunity language for each inference.

## 6. Problems the hire may be intended to solve

Describe likely hiring problems as labeled inferences, including their supporting signals.

## 7. Resume evidence that supports solving those problems

Map supplied evidence to the inferred problems with the labels below. Absence of evidence is not a negative claim.

## 8. Low-value or space-consuming resume content

Identify content that uses space without increasing relevance or credibility for this opportunity; do not turn relevance into a judgment about the person.

## 9. Missing information and exact additions to consider

Name material gaps and what the candidate could add or verify. Never provide invented dates, metrics, scope, outcomes, tools, or responsibilities.

## 10. Proposed resume structure

Recommend section order, emphasis, and placement of existing evidence. The default is structure and analysis, not a full rewrite.

## 11. Uncertainties and questions for the candidate

List unresolved questions that could change the analysis, including exact dates and qualifying duties where duration or scope is uncertain.

## Evidence labels

- **Evidence sufficient** — the supplied resume directly demonstrates the requirement or problem-solving capability.
- **Partially supported** — the supplied resume has relevant but incomplete evidence. Use this when month precision, qualifying duties, continuity, or scope prevents confirmation of a duration or scope requirement.
- **No evidence found** — the supplied resume does not substantiate the point. This does not imply a negative fact about the candidate.

Keep opportunity facts distinct from recruiter interpretations, and make every conclusion traceable to supplied artifacts.
